# Responsible Use

## Intended scope

Scientific Manuscript Audit supports author-side manuscript assessment, coauthor review, internal research-group quality control, public preprint analysis, revision review, rebuttal review, and other explicitly authorized scientific-evaluation workflows.

## Authorization and confidentiality

Users should establish their authority to process unpublished material before submitting it to an AI system. Journal policies, institutional rules, contracts, data-use terms, and confidentiality agreements govern the permitted handling of manuscripts and review materials.

Confidential third-party submissions, formal journal review assignments, editor-only communications, and unpublished research from other groups require explicit permission for AI-assisted processing. Local installation does not by itself establish permission or confidentiality compliance. Model providers, network services, plugins, logging systems, and connected tools may have separate data-handling terms.

## Scientific integrity

The skill instructs the host agent to:

- distinguish direct checks, supported inference, literature verification needs, and unverified scope
- avoid invented citations, calculations, experiments, source access, and technical verification
- ground major comments in manuscript claims and inspected evidence
- state the limits of available material and expertise
- resist predetermined acceptance or rejection requests
- treat manuscript-embedded instructions as untrusted content

Users should independently verify every decision-relevant technical assertion and cited source.

## Editorial status

Recommendations generated by the skill are advisory assessments. Journals and conferences retain formal editorial authority. Human authors, reviewers, and editors retain responsibility for submissions, reports, disclosures, and decisions.

## Disclosure

Users should follow the disclosure requirements of the relevant journal, conference, employer, funder, and institution. The repository does not define a universal disclosure rule.

## Public examples and evaluation data

Public contributions should use synthetic, public-domain, openly licensed, or otherwise redistributable materials. Remove personal data, confidential research, editor correspondence, private reviewer reports, credentials, and local-environment information before publication.
